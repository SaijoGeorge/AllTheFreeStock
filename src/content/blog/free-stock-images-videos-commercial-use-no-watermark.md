## The short answer

If you want a full-size file with no watermark, no credit line and a licence that covers ads and client work, start with **Pexels, Unsplash, Pixabay, StockSnap and Burst** for photos and **Pexels, Pixabay, Coverr and Mixkit** for video. We read each site's own licence page in October 2026, and every one of them lets you download the original file for free with no attribution required.

Most other sites calling themselves free fall into one of three groups:

- **Free with a credit.** Magnific (the platform formerly called Freepik) and Vecteezy give away files without a watermark, but their free licences require attribution and cap what you can do commercially.
- **Free previews of paid files.** Adobe Stock, iStock and Shutterstock let you download watermarked comps for mock-ups. Those files carry no right to publish.
- **Mixed libraries.** Mixkit hosts free and restricted clips side by side, so the licence changes from one clip to the next.

The rest of this guide shows which licence sits behind each download, what "commercial use" covers, and the handful of things no free licence allows. If you're new to licence types, our guide to [free stock photo licences](https://allthefreestock.com/blog/free-stock-photo-licences-explained/) explains CC0, custom licences and editorial-only terms first.

## Which sites give clean files with no credit?

Eight sites offer free, full files with no watermark and no attribution requirement. We checked these against each site's own licence page in October 2026. Two of the biggest, Pexels and Unsplash, are often described as CC0. Neither is. Both now use their own licences, which are just as generous for normal commercial work but add a few rules of their own.

| Site | Media | Watermark on free download | Credit needed | Commercial use | Notable restriction |
| - | - | - | - | - | - |
| [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/license/) | Photos, video | No | No | Yes | No selling unaltered copies; no use in a trademark |
| [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/license) | Photos | No | No | Yes | No prints or products for sale; no competing service |
| [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/) | Photos, video, music | No | No | Yes | No standalone resale; visible brands not for goods |
| [StockSnap](https://stocksnap.io/license) | Photos | No | No | Yes (`CC0`) | Don't imply endorsement |
| [Burst](https://www.shopify.com/stock-photos/licenses/shopify-some-rights-reserved) | Photos | No | No | Yes | No selling photos in any form |
| [Coverr](https://coverr.co/license) | Video, music | No | No | Yes | No redistribution; no AI training |
| [Mixkit](https://mixkit.co/license/) (Free licence) | Video, music, SFX | No | No | Yes | Restricted-licence items are personal only |
| [Adobe Stock free collection](https://stock.adobe.com/free) | Photos, video, vectors | No | No | Yes | Download cap; Standard licence limits apply |

Sources: each site's licence page, linked in the table.

A few details in those licences matter more than the table can show:

- **Pexels** bans showing identifiable people "in a bad light or in a way that is offensive", implying endorsement by people or brands in the image, selling unaltered copies, using the media in a trademark or business name, and redistributing it on other stock or wallpaper platforms.
- **Unsplash** grants an "irrevocable, nonexclusive, worldwide copyright license" for commercial use. The only restriction on its licence page is compiling images "to replicate a similar or competing service". Its help centre adds that it does not offer a licence for selling prints or products with images printed on them.
- **Pixabay** forbids selling or distributing content "on a Standalone basis", which it defines as content with no creative effort applied. Its full [Content Licence terms](https://pixabay.com/service/terms/) also exclude third-party sponsored results from the licence, so a sponsored tile in Pixabay search isn't covered.
- **Burst** says you are "not permitted to sell any License Photos as digital photo files or in any other form", which is stricter than Pexels or Pixabay.
- **Coverr** bans using its videos or music to train AI models, and says it doesn't pass on the model releases it holds.

> **Rule of thumb:** "No watermark" is about the file. "No credit" and "commercial use" are about the licence. Check all three on the licence page itself, because third-party lists often get at least one wrong.

## Freemium sites: where the catch sits

On freemium sites, the catch sits in the licence terms or in the paywall, even when the free file itself is clean. Some put a credit requirement on free files, some put usage caps on them, and some only give you a watermarked preview until you pay.

| Site | What "free" gets you | Watermark | Credit | Key limits on the free tier |
| - | - | - | - | - |
| Magnific (formerly Freepik) | Free-licence assets | Not on free assets | Required | Daily download limits; no resale products |
| Vecteezy | Free-licence assets | Not on free assets | Required | 50 print copies; US$1,000 video budget; no resale products |
| Adobe Stock (free collection) | Selected assets | No | No | Download cap per period |
| Adobe Stock (paid assets) | Low-res preview | Yes | n/a | Preview only until licensed |
| iStock | Comp preview | Yes | n/a | Layouts only, 30 days, never public |
| Shutterstock | 3 selected free photos with a free account | Previews of paid files | No | Standard licence terms |
| Unsplash+ | Nothing free | n/a | No | Paid subscription only |

### Magnific (formerly Freepik)

Freepik rebranded as Magnific on 28 April 2026 ([Tech.eu, April 2026](https://tech.eu/2026/04/28/freepik-rebrands-as-magnific-unifying-its-ai-creative-stack-as-enterprise-and-no-collar-growth-accelerates/)). The old Videvo free video site now redirects to Magnific's video section too, so clips you used to grab from Videvo fall under Magnific's licence. Its [licences and attribution page](https://www.magnific.com/ai/docs/licenses-attribution) is blunt: free-licence assets, videos included, need attribution "always", and you need a Premium, Premium+ or Pro subscription to drop the credit.

The [terms of use](https://www.magnific.com/legal/terms-of-use) add more. Section 4 sets daily download limits for free users. Section 8.1 bars using content as the main element of items "aimed to be resold", bars reselling or sublicensing the files, and bars using them in a trademark unless you've changed them into a new work. Physical products for commercial sale only open up on Pro, capped at 100,000 units per asset (section 9.3.4).

### Vecteezy

Vecteezy's [licensing agreement](https://www.vecteezy.com/licensing-agreement) spells out the Free licence in numbers. Attribution is mandatory, and if you can't credit the creator, "you must upgrade to a Pro Standard License". The Free licence also:

- bans use "on products for resale"
- caps print runs at 50 copies
- caps video and film production budgets at US$1,000
- limits Vecteezy's legal guarantee to US$500 per claim

Those caps rule out most real business use: a flyer run of 500, a client video with a modest budget, or packaging.

### Adobe Stock, iStock and Shutterstock

These are paid libraries with a free corner. Adobe's [Stock FAQ](https://helpx.adobe.com/stock/faq.html) says assets in its free collection come with "a royalty-free commercial-use license", the Standard licence for photos and the Enhanced licence for video. Everything else can be downloaded as a "low-resolution, watermarked" preview, which you pay to license.

iStock's [licence agreement](https://www.istockphoto.com/legal/license-agreement) states that watermarked files are "for test or sample layout (composite) use only", "cannot be used in any final materials or any publicly available materials", and "may only be used for the 30 days following download". Shutterstock's [licence](https://www.shutterstock.com/license) says the same of comp video, which may not be incorporated "into any final materials".

Shutterstock ended its 10-image free trial on 1 March 2024. At the time of writing (October 2026) a free account gets three selected free photos under the Standard licence and a set of curated free videos ([Photutorial, updated September 2026](https://photutorial.com/shutterstock-ends-free-trial)).

> **Common mistake:** Dropping a watermarked comp into a client draft, getting sign-off, and forgetting to license it before launch. Name comp files with a `_COMP` suffix so they're easy to catch before anything goes live.

### Unsplash+ and premium tiers inside free sites

Unsplash runs a paid tier, Unsplash+, alongside its free library. At launch in October 2022, Unsplash said all Unsplash+ images were model and property released and backed by expanded legal protection ([Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/blog/introducing-unsplash)). If you use both, note in your asset log which licence each file came under.

## What does "commercial use" actually cover?

Commercial use means any use that promotes a business or makes money: ads, websites, social posts, sales decks, client projects and monetised video. All eight free sites in the first table allow it. The limits start when the stock image itself becomes the thing you're selling.

| Use | Pexels, Unsplash, Pixabay | Watch out for |
| - | - | - |
| Website hero, blog image | Yes | Nothing special |
| Paid social and search ads | Yes | Implied endorsement by people in the shot |
| Client work (agency, freelance) | Yes | Don't hand over raw files as a stock pack |
| Monetised YouTube, Reels, TikTok | Yes | Content ID claims on popular clips |
| Brochures, flyers, packaging | Yes | Visible brands; Vecteezy's 50-copy cap |
| Merch, prints, mugs | Pexels and Pixabay only if altered | Unsplash and Burst don't allow it |
| Logo or business name | No | Banned on Pexels, Pixabay and most others |
| Templates or apps you sell | Risky | Counts as redistribution on many licences |

Two points from the licences explain most of that table:

1. **The image must be part of something bigger.** Pexels says "don't sell unaltered copies", and Pixabay defines unlawful "standalone" use as content where "no creative effort has been applied". A poster of an unedited Pexels photo fails both tests. A book cover with your own type, layout and other elements usually passes.
2. **The licence covers the photographer's copyright, not everyone else's rights.** Pexels' own help centre warns that trademarks, logos or brands shown in a work "may still be protected by privacy, copyright or trademark rights" ([Pexels Help](https://help.pexels.com/hc/en-us/articles/360042295214-Can-I-use-the-photos-and-videos-for-a-commercial-project)).

> **Quick check:** Ask "is the image the product, or part of the product?" If a buyer would be paying mainly for the photo, you need a print or merchandise licence, which most free sites don't offer.

## What's still off-limits under permissive licences?

Even the most generous free licences leave five things off-limits: implying endorsement by recognisable people, using logos or trademarks commercially, reselling unaltered files, turning the image into your own trademark, and building a competing library. These rules exist because the photographer can only license their own copyright.

### Recognisable people and endorsement

A free licence never grants a person's consent to endorse your product. Pexels bans implying endorsement by people or brands in the image. StockSnap says the same. Pixabay bans "immoral or illegal" use, "especially Content which features recognisable people".

In practice, a smiling stranger on your homepage is fine. The same stranger in an ad captioned "I lost 10 kilos with this app", or next to a political message, a health condition or a testimonial, needs a model release that names that use. Shutterstock's paid licence restricts the same sensitive uses, including portraying a model as suffering from an ailment or in a political context.

### Logos, trademarks and private property

Pixabay's licence says content showing recognisable trademarks, logos or brands can't be used commercially for goods or services, including merchandise. Coverr says it doesn't hold releases for "brands, trademarks, or identifiable properties". If a laptop logo or a café sign is sharp and readable, retouch it out or pick another shot. Our post on [stock photos that aren't AI-generated](https://allthefreestock.com/blog/stock-photos-not-ai-generated/) covers checking a file's origin, which helps when you need to show where an image came from.

### Reselling or redistributing the file

Every licence we read bans passing the original file on as a stock asset. Pexels names "other stock photo or wallpaper platforms", Coverr names website builders, theme providers and app builders, and Magnific's terms bar including its files in databases or stock libraries for distribution.

### Using the image as your trademark

Pexels and Pixabay both ban using content "as part of a trade-mark, design-mark, trade-name, business name or service mark". If you're designing a logo, start from scratch or license a vector that explicitly allows logo use.

### AI training and scraping

This is newer. Coverr's licence bans using its content to "train AI algorithms, develop AI models", and Pixabay's terms prohibit scraping the service "for machine learning purposes". Downloading a few images for a campaign is fine. Bulk-downloading a library to fine-tune a model isn't.

## Never remove a watermark

Removing a watermark to avoid paying for an image is copyright infringement, and in the US, UK and Australia it can also breach separate laws that protect copyright management information. You carry both liabilities, and "it was only a preview" is no defence.

A watermark usually names the agency or the photographer, which puts it inside the legal definition of rights information:

- **United States.** [17 U.S.C. § 1202](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1202) bans intentionally removing or altering copyright management information, which includes the author's and owner's names and identifying symbols, where you know it will help or hide infringement. Under [§ 1203](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/1203), the owner can claim statutory damages of "not less than $2,500 or more than $25,000" for each violation, on top of the infringement claim. In 2011 the Third Circuit held that a printed photo credit counts as copyright management information even outside an automated system ([Murphy v. Millennium Radio Group](https://copyrightalliance.org/copyright-cases/murphy-v-millennium-radio)).
- **Australia.** Under [section 132AQ](https://20.austlii.edu.au/au/legis/cth/consol_act/ca1968133/s132aq.html) of the Copyright Act 1968, removing or altering electronic rights management information without permission, where it will facilitate or conceal infringement, carries up to 550 penalty units, five years' imprisonment, or both. With the Commonwealth penalty unit at $330 for offences from 7 November 2024 ([AFSA](https://www.afsa.gov.au/professionals/resource-hub/penalty-units)), that's a maximum fine of $181,500 for an individual, and corporations face up to five times the fine. The [Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1968A00063/latest/text) also gives copyright owners a civil action for the same conduct in section 116B. The [Copyright Agency](https://www.copyright.com.au/?p=27317) notes that the Act also prohibits commercial dealing with material whose rights information has been removed.
- **United Kingdom.** [Section 296ZG](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/296ZG) of the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 gives rightsholders the same remedies as for infringement against anyone who knowingly removes rights information, or distributes copies with it removed.

Watermarks can also be trade marks. In November 2025, the UK High Court found that a small number of Stable Diffusion v1 and v2 outputs showing Getty and iStock watermarks infringed those marks, though the finding was "extremely limited" and most of Getty's claims failed ([Bird & Bird, November 2025](https://cm.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/uk/stability-ai-defeats-getty-images-copyright-claims-in-first-of-its-kind-dispute-before-the-high-cour)).

Image owners do pursue these claims. Pictorial, graphic and sculptural works made up 36% of the 1,920 claims filed with the US Copyright Claims Board between June 2022 and March 2026 ([CCB, April 2026](https://www.ccb.gov/CCB-Statistics-and-FAQs-April-2026.pdf)), the single largest category.

> **Watch out:** "Watermark remover" tools, AI inpainting and cropping all count as removal. Some tutorials for free video editors suggest trimming a watermark off the end of a clip. If a file has a watermark you didn't put there, license it or find a free alternative.

## Free video without watermarks

For commercial video with no watermark and no credit, Pexels, Pixabay, Coverr and Mixkit's Free licence are the safest starting points. Videvo, long a staple of these lists, now redirects to Magnific, where free clips need a credit. The watermark problem in video usually comes from editing apps that stamp their logo on exports, and you avoid that by downloading the source clip from the stock site.

| Site | Licence on free clips | Credit | Watch for |
| - | - | - | - |
| Pexels | Pexels Licence | No | Recognisable people, visible brands |
| Pixabay | Pixabay Content Licence | No | Sponsored third-party results |
| Coverr | Coverr Licence | No | No AI training; no resale |
| Mixkit | Stock Video Free Licence | No | Restricted-licence clips are non-commercial |
| Magnific (formerly Videvo) | Magnific Free licence | Yes | Daily download limits; no resale |

Mixkit's Free licence covers commercial projects such as YouTube videos, social media marketing, online ads and music videos, while its Restricted licence is limited to personal, non-commercial projects. The two sit in the same library, so read the licence label on each clip.

## Check before you download

Before you download a file for a commercial project, open the licence page, confirm the four conditions below, and save a record. It takes about a minute per file and makes any later claim much easier to answer.

| Check | Where to look | Pass if |
| - | - | - |
| File is the free original | Download button, file size | Full resolution, no overlay |
| Licence allows commercial use | Site's licence page | Says commercial, not editorial-only or restricted |
| No credit required, or you can give one | Licence page or item label | Matches your use (ads rarely have room) |
