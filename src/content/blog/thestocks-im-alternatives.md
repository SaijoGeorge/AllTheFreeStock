## What happened to TheStocks.im?

TheStocks.im is still online, but it no longer does the job it was famous for. At the time of writing (October 2026), the domain loads "The Stocks 3", a directory of 2,100+ design tools run by Panda Network, and you can't browse free stock photo sites through it the way you could in 2015.

So if a bookmark or an old blog post sent you to thestocks.im and you found icon finders, Notion templates and job boards where the photo sites used to be, the site is working as intended. Its owners changed its purpose.

Here's what we found when we checked the live site, its archive history and its launch pages:

| Date | Version | What it was |
| - | - | - |
| 21 Aug 2014 | The Stocks (v1) | "The best royalty free stock photos in one place" |
| 14 Dec 2016 | The Stocks v2 | Photos plus colours, icons, mockups and more |
| 10 Aug 2022 | TheStocks v3 | "2100+ daily updating tools for designers" |
| Oct 2026 | The Stocks 3 (live) | Design tools directory, 35 categories |

Sources: [Product Hunt launches](https://www.producthunt.com/products/the-stocks/launches), [Wayback Machine capture, January 2015](https://web.archive.org/web/20150103202349/http://thestocks.im/), and our own check of [thestocks.im](https://thestocks.im/) on 31 October 2026.

### What the site shows today

When we loaded thestocks.im on 31 October 2026, it returned a working page. The page title still reads "The Stocks 3 | Best royalty free stock photos, videos, mockups, icons and fonts!", and the meta description promises "2100+ tools for Designers" that "Updates daily".

The page itself is a sidebar of 35 categories. Stock Photos and Stock Videos are two of them, next to Color Tools, For Figma, Icon Sets, Design Systems, Prototyping, Job Boards, Designer Podcasts and others. The footer says "Made by Panda Network", the feedback links go to a [Panda](https://usepanda.com/) email address, and the page embeds a sidebar promoting other [Panda Network](https://panda.network/) properties and sponsors.

Two details suggest the directory isn't maintained day to day, whatever the meta description says:

- The "Recently Added" list on the homepage includes an entry called "Best Hacktoberfest 2023 Projects".
- The "Newsletter Inbox" panel shows twelve sample issues dated 6 to 17 July 2022, with a note that the free newsletter is "launching soon".

> **Quick check:** The "Updates daily" claim appears only in the page's metadata, and the visible entries are older. When you judge whether any directory is current, trust the newest dated item on the page over the tagline.

### Who made it

[Product Hunt credits](https://www.producthunt.com/products/the-stocks/makers) Ahmet Sülek, founder of the Panda news reader, as the maker, along with five teammates including developers and a product designer. The January 2015 version of the site already carried a Panda logo in its footer linking to usepanda.com, so the two have been connected from the start.

Sülek's own comments on the [v3 launch thread](https://www.producthunt.com/p/the-stocks/thestocks-v3) explain the scale of the change. He wrote that the first version took "maybe 3 hours and one person", while v3 took a team almost three months. He also described the stack: a SvelteJS front end with Google Sheets as the database, hosted on Google Cloud Storage. On the original 2014 [launch thread](https://www.producthunt.com/p/the-stocks/the-stocks), he said he'd built the list from a Medium article and kept only sites with "relatively better design".

### What we couldn't confirm

We couldn't confirm why the photo-focused version was retired, or whether the team plans further changes. We found no blog post, announcement or social media statement from the makers about it. The v3 launch post frames the change as an expansion to all design resources, not a shutdown. We also couldn't confirm exact traffic figures. [Semrush's third-party estimate](https://www.semrush.com/website/thestocks.im/overview/) puts September 2026 visits at about 42,000, down from about 75,000 in July, but those are modelled numbers.

## What TheStocks originally offered

The original TheStocks was a single page with a list of free photo sites down the left and the selected site loaded in a frame on the right. You clicked "Pexels" or "Gratisography", the site opened inside TheStocks, and you browsed without juggling tabs.

It didn't host photos or check licences, and at launch it had no search of its own. It showed other people's libraries in one window, which was the appeal in 2014, when free photo sites were mostly small Tumblr blogs with no search at all. One commenter on the 2014 launch thread pointed out there was no search feature, and Sülek replied that search would come.

The v2 relaunch in December 2016 widened the scope. The [Wayback Machine's 2018 capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20180104165826/http://thestocks.im/) carries the title "The Stocks 2 - Best royalty free stock photos, videos, mockups, icons and fonts", and the v2 launch reached #1 Product of the Day on Product Hunt with 412 upvotes ([Hunted.space](https://webmail.hunted.space/product/the-stocks/launches/the-stocks-v2)). By v3 in 2022, photos had become two categories among dozens.

> **Worth knowing:** The Internet Archive holds more than 1,350 captures of the TheStocks homepage, from 22 August 2014 to 22 September 2026. If you're trying to recover a source you found there years ago, the early captures still show the full list.

## Where its original 14 sources are now

The January 2015 capture lists 14 photo sites. We checked every one in October 2026. Six still run as photo libraries, three are online but frozen, four are gone or now host something unrelated, and one we couldn't confirm.

| Original source | Status (Oct 2026) | What we found |
| - | - | - |
| [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/) | Live library | Major library, own licence |
| [Little Visuals](https://littlevisuals.co/) | Online, frozen | Kept by the founder's family since 2013 |
| [New Old Stock](https://nos.twnsnd.co/) | Live library | Vintage archive photos on Tumblr |
| Super Famous | Repurposed | Redirects to the creator's design portfolio |
| [Startup Stock Photos](https://startupstockphotos.com/) | Live library | Office and tech photos, still online |
| [Gratisography](https://gratisography.com/) | Live library | Quirky photos, new weekly |
| Getrefe | Couldn't confirm | Tumblr wouldn't load; getrefe.com is a coupon site |
| Crow the Stone | Gone | Default hosting placeholder page |
| [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/) | Live library | Major library, own licence |
| Jay Mantri | Repurposed | Personal design portfolio, no photo downloads |
| [Magdeleine](https://magdeleine.co/) | Live library | Curated CC0 and CC photos |
| Breaking Pic | Gone | Domain no longer resolves |
| Travel Coffee Book | Online, frozen | Newest post we saw: September 2019 |
| [Moveast](https://moveast.me/) | Online, frozen | CC0 travel photos, posts about 10 years old |

A few of these deserve a note:

- **Little Visuals** says its founder, Nic Jackson, died suddenly in November 2013, and that his family has kept the images available and free for commercial use. Nothing new will be added, but the archive is intact.
- **Super Famous** and **Jay Mantri** were photographers' personal sites. The domains still belong to working designers, but the free photo sections are gone.
- **Travel Coffee Book** moved from Tumblr to its own domain. When we checked, one post's download link pointed to an unrelated shopping domain. That's the risk with frozen sites: nobody is watching the links.

That's why an old list like TheStocks' can't be used as-is. In about a decade, four of its 14 original sources disappeared or turned into something else, three stopped updating, and the ones that survived are mostly the big, company-backed libraries.

> **Common mistake:** Following links from a 2015 "best free stock photo sites" article without checking where they go. An expired domain can be bought by anyone, and a link that once led to CC0 photos can lead to a spam or malware page today.

## The best TheStocks.im alternatives in 2026

The best alternative depends on which part of TheStocks you miss. If you want one search box across many free photo sites, use Openverse or Everypixel. If you want a browsable, checked list of sites across photos, video, icons and fonts, use a curated directory.

### AllTheFreeStock

This is the site you're reading, so weigh this section accordingly. AllTheFreeStock started in 2015 as a personal bookmarks list, the same era as TheStocks, and it does a similar job in a different way: it's a hand-curated directory of free photos, video, audio, icons, fonts, mockups and templates, with 270 resources across 9 categories at the time of writing.

Each entry carries a plain-language licence tag (such as CC0, site licence or mixed), dead or hijacked links get removed, and anything without a genuinely free tier is marked as paid. It doesn't search inside other sites, and it doesn't frame them. You browse [our free stock directory](https://allthefreestock.com/), pick a source and go to it directly.

### Everypixel free search

[Everypixel's free search](https://www.everypixel.com/free) is the closest thing to a working "search all the free sites" box. It says it searches "22 stock image websites in one place" and updates its index every week. It also offers search by image, which helps when you have a reference shot and want a free lookalike.

The page describes the photos as being under Creative Commons 0. Several of the big sources it draws from use their own licences these days, not CC0, so click through to the original page and read the licence there.

### Openverse

[Openverse](https://openverse.org/) is the open-licence search engine that replaced CC Search. Creative Commons announced the move to WordPress in 2021, and on 13 December 2021 CC Search began redirecting to Openverse ([Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/2021/12/13/dear-users-of-cc-search-welcome-to-openverse/)). Its [about page](https://openverse.org/about) says it indexes more than 800 million images and audio tracks.

Openverse's strength is licence filtering: you can narrow results to works that allow commercial use or modification, and each result has ready-made attribution text. Its weakness is quality. Results come from museums, Flickr and many other sources, so you'll scroll past a lot of snapshots before you find a polished stock-style image. Openverse itself tells users to verify licence information at the source.

### Stock Up

[Stock Up](https://stockup.sitebuilderreport.com/) was one of TheStocks' original rivals and it's still online. It says it combines photos "with permission" from about 25 sites into one search covering 25,000+ images, and it links back to each source. When we checked, its "latest" page showed 0 photos added that week, and the newest images on its homepage carry 2021 and 2022 upload timestamps. It's useful for a fast CC0 search, but the index is ageing.

### Wikimedia Commons

[Wikimedia Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Main_Page) describes itself as "a collection of 50,733,117 freely usable media files" at the time of writing. It's the go-to for real places, buildings, objects, historical events and public figures, which general stock libraries cover poorly. Every file has a description page with its licence, author and source. Licences vary from public domain to CC BY-SA, so read each one.

### Flickr Commons

[The Commons on Flickr](https://www.flickr.com/commons) shares photography from cultural institutions. It launched in 2008 with the Library of Congress and now has over 100 member institutions. Most images carry the label "no known copyright restrictions", which [Flickr explains](https://www.flickr.com/commons/usage/) is an institution's reasonable conclusion, not a warranty. It's excellent for historical and archival imagery.

### Google Images usage rights filter

Google Images has a [Usage rights filter](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/29508) with two options: Creative Commons licences, and Commercial or other licences. It searches the whole web, which makes it the widest net on this list, but Google relies on licence data from the hosting sites and tells you to confirm the licence yourself. Use it to find candidates, then verify on the source page.

### Unsplash, Pexels and Pixabay as starting points

If what you really want is a big, searchable library of modern photos, skip the aggregators and go straight to [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/), [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/) or [Pixabay](https://pixabay.com/). Pixabay's homepage says it hosts more than 6.3 million assets, including photos, illustrations, vectors, video, music and sound effects. All three have strong search and filters, and all three appeared on TheStocks at some point.

Their licences are their own, and each has limits. The [Unsplash licence](https://unsplash.com/license) "does not include the right to compile images from Unsplash to replicate a similar or competing service." The [Pexels licence](https://www.pexels.com/license/) says not to sell unaltered copies or redistribute the photos on other stock or wallpaper platforms, and [Pixabay's summary](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/) bars selling or distributing content on a "Standalone" basis. Those clauses help explain why so few cross-site aggregators survive: the big libraries don't want their catalogues rebuilt elsewhere. For a fuller breakdown, see our guide to [free stock photo licences](https://allthefreestock.com/blog/free-stock-photo-licences-explained/).

### Two that changed

Some sites that appear on older "TheStocks alternatives" lists have changed what they do:

- **LibreStock.** The [2016 Wayback capture](https://web.archive.org/web/20161226214115/http://librestock.com/) describes it as a search engine that "scans and indexes the stock photos from 40+ different websites". Today [LibreStock](https://librestock.com/) releases 10 exclusive CC0 photos a month, and the cross-site search is gone.
- **VisualHunt.** Once a search engine for free photos, [VisualHunt](https://visualhunt.com/)'s homepage now promotes Foter's background remover and photo tools.

> **Watch out:** Most "alternatives to TheStocks" articles were written between 2015 and 2018 and haven't been checked since. If a list still recommends LibreStock or VisualHunt as multi-site search engines, it's out of date.

## How to vet a free stock directory

Before you trust any directory or aggregator, TheStocks or otherwise, run through these checks. They take about two minutes and catch most problems.

| Check | What good looks like | Red flag |
| - | - | - |
| Newest dated item | Added in the last few months | Newest entry is years old |
| Link health | Click 5 random links; all load the right site | Parked pages, errors, unrelated businesses |
| Licence info | Licence named per entry or per file | "All free" with no detail |
| Paid vs free labels | Freemium and paid sites marked | Paid sites mixed in unlabelled |
| Who runs it | Named curator or company, contact route | Anonymous, no contact |
| Source attribution | Links straight to the original page | Re-hosts files with no source |
| Removal policy | Says how dead links get removed | No sign anyone prunes it |
| Ads and sponsors | Sponsored slots labelled | Ads disguised as listings |

Two habits sit on top of the table.

1. **Download from the source, never the middleman.** Click through to the original library and download there. That puts you on the page whose licence actually applies, and you avoid files that have been re-saved or stripped of metadata.
2. **Record what you used.** Save the source URL, the licence name and the date for every asset. If a site later changes its terms or vanishes, the way so many of TheStocks' sources did, you still have your evidence.

> **Rule of thumb:** A directory is only as current as its last pruning. If you can't find a date anywhere on the page, assume nobody has checked the links recently.

AI answer engines face the same problem when they recommend resources: they lean on pages that show their dates, sources and status checks openly. ## Where that leaves TheStocks

TheStocks.im did something useful in 2014: it put a dozen scattered free photo blogs in one window when most of them had no search. The free stock world has since consolidated around a few large libraries and open-licence search engines, and TheStocks moved on to design tools in general.

If you miss the original, combine three things: one cross-site search (Openverse or Everypixel), one or two big libraries you know well, and a checked directory for everything that isn't a photo. You can find all of these and more in [the free resources we've curated](https://allthefreestock.com/), each with its licence noted.
