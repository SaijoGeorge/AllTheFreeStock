faqs:
  - q: "How can I tell if a free stock photo site is still maintained?"
    a: "Look at the newest uploads, not the copyright year in the footer. Many sites update the footer automatically while nothing new gets added. Open the latest photo and check its upload date, or look at image file paths: on the site's homepage when we checked, [Public Domain Archive](https://www.publicdomainarchive.com/) still claimed new photos weekly, but its newest image folders dated from 2017. A blog or social account that went silent years ago, a broken licence page, or a homepage taken over by affiliate offers are other signs."
  - q: "How do I find the licence of a stock site that has gone offline?"
    a: "Search for the site on the Wayback Machine at the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) and open a snapshot of its licence or terms page from around the date you downloaded. Save that snapshot's URL in your asset records. If the site was CC0, the image file page may also appear in Openverse or Wikimedia Commons with the licence attached. When no snapshot exists, treat the image as unverified and replace it in anything commercial."
  - q: "Do I need to update photo credits that link to a dead stock site?"
    a: "It's good practice. A credit linking to a parked or redirected domain can send readers somewhere unrelated, and broken outbound links make a page look neglected. For CC BY images, attribution is a licence condition, so keep the photographer's name and the licence link even if the site is gone. Point the link to the photographer's own portfolio or an archived copy of the original page instead."
  - q: "What happened to Videvo and Mazwai free videos?"

At the time of writing, [Magnific's video library](https://www.magnific.com/videos) still has free clips, but free use requires crediting the creator. Our [free stock footage guide](https://allthefreestock.com/blog/best-free-stock-footage-sites/) covers other options."
  - q: "Is Unsplash going to stop being free?"
    a: "There's no sign of it. When Unsplash launched its paid Unsplash+ tier in October 2022, it said its \"open library isn't going anywhere\" ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2022/10/10/unsplash-adds-a-paid-tier-one-year-after-getty-takeover/)). The free library had grown to 5 million images by April 2023 ([Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/history)). Paid images are mixed into search results, though, so check for the Unsplash+ badge before you download."
  - q: "Are Pexels and Pixabay safe bets now that Canva owns them?"
    a: "They are among the safest free libraries available. Canva bought both in May 2019 and said the photos would stay free for everyone ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/17/australias-design-unicorn-canva-picks-up-two-free-image-sharing-services-and-launches-new-photo-product)). If you have old bookmarks or tutorials pointing to CC Search, update them. Openverse searches openly licensed images and audio across many collections, and shows the licence for each result."
  - q: "What happens to my uploads if a stock site I contribute to closes?"
    a: "It depends on the site's terms, so read the closure notice closely. When EyeEm closed in January 2026, photographers could opt in to move their work to Freepik. Anyone who didn't had their images removed from EyeEm and its partner agencies. Licences already sold stayed valid ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2025/12/11/eyeem-is-shutting-down/)). Keep your own originals and a list of where each photo is published."
  - q: "Can someone rescue a closed free stock site's photos?"
    a: "If the photos were released under CC0, yes. Anyone can re-host them, because CC0 waives copyright permanently. Photos under a custom licence like Unsplash's are different: those licences usually ban compiling the images into a competing service, so a mirror needs the owner's permission."

## Which free stock photo sites have shut down?

Very few free stock photo sites close with an announcement. Most go quiet, get bought, change their licence or start redirecting to a paid library. We checked more than 30 once-popular free photo and video sites. The table shows where each one stood at the end of October 2026.

| Site | Known for | Status (Oct 2026) | Change | Go here instead |
| - | - | - | - | - |
| stock.xchng / FreeImages | 400,000+ free photos, 2001 onwards | Redirects to iStock | 2009 sale; redirect by 2026 | Pexels, Unsplash |
| 500px Creative Commons | 1M+ CC-licensed photos | Closed | 2018 | Openverse, Wikimedia Commons |
| TheStocks.im | Aggregator of free photo sites | Repurposed as tools directory | Relaunched as v3 | Our free stock directory |
| CC Search | Search across CC images | Redirects to Openverse | 2021 | Openverse |
| Jay Mantri | Nature and city photography | Closed (personal page) | Unknown | Unsplash |
| Superfamous | Abstract and nature, CC BY | Closed (studio portfolio) | Unknown | [Magdeleine](https://magdeleine.co/) |
| Little Visuals | Free high-res photo packs | Dormant, kept as memorial | Founder died 2013 | Unsplash |
| New Old Stock | Vintage public domain photos | Dormant | Last post 2022 | Library of Congress |
| Travel Coffee Book | Travel photos | Dormant | Last post 2019 | Unsplash, Pexels |
| Public Domain Archive | CC0 vintage and modern | Dormant | Newest files 2017 | Smithsonian Open Access |
| morgueFile | Free reference photos | Homepage now sells iStock | Unknown | Pixabay |
| Death to the Stock Photo | Free monthly email packs | Went paid | Membership by 2015 | Pexels, Kaboompics |
| Videvo, Mazwai | Free stock video | Redirect to Magnific | 2022 sale, 2026 rebrand | Pexels Videos |
| Nappy | Photos of Black and brown people | Closing within months | Announced 2026 | See below |
| Burst by Shopify | Product and ecommerce photos | Moved, still free | Redirect to shopify.com | Same library |
| Foodiesfeed | Food photography, CC0 | Live, up for sale | 2026 | Download what you need |

Status labels mean what they say. **Closed** means no free photos are served at the old address. **Dormant** means the site loads but nothing new has been added in years. **Went paid** means the free offer has been replaced by a subscription. **Redirects** means the domain now sends you somewhere else.

> **Quick check:** A footer that says "© 2026" proves nothing. Plenty of dormant sites update the year automatically. Look at the date of the newest upload instead.

## Sites that closed or now redirect

These are the sites where the original free library is no longer available at its old address.

### stock.xchng and FreeImages

stock.xchng (`sxc.hu`) launched in February 2001 and was one of the first big free photo sites ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FreeImages)). By 2009 it held more than 400,000 free images, and Getty Images bought it that July ([Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/?p=4186)). iStock's COO at the time told [Selling Stock](https://www.selling-stock.com/Article/istock-welcomes-stockxchng-to-getty-images-fa) that free sites were "a great marketing vehicle", meaning a way to upsell users into paid iStock content. In August 2013, with more than 2.5 million registered users, the site went down for days behind a maintenance notice ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2013/08/06/stock-photo-service-stock-xchng-down-for-days-users-left-in-the-dark)). It was renamed FreeImages in 2014.

When we checked on 31 October 2026, both `freeimages.com` and its licence page returned a `302` redirect to the [iStock](https://www.istockphoto.com/) homepage. We couldn't find a public announcement or an exact date for the change.

### 500px Creative Commons

In July 2018, 500px closed its own marketplace, moved licensing to Getty Images and VCG, and removed Creative Commons licensing altogether. More than a million free images became impossible to search for or download on the site ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/500px-has-killed-off-its-stock-photo-marketplace-and-creative-commons-licensing-options)). 500px still runs as a photo community. Its free library is gone.

### Nappy

Nappy built a library of free photos of Black and brown people, released into the public domain. In October 2026 it posted that it would close "in the next few months", saying demand for authentic images "fell off a cliff" and that it wouldn't add an AI face generator ([Nappy](https://nappy.co/future), reported by [kottke.org](https://kottke.org/26/10/0049739-nappy) on 5 October 2026). Downloads stay open until the final day, and the team says photos already downloaded remain under CC0. It also asked anyone interested in taking the site over to get in touch.

> **Watch out:** If you rely on Nappy for diverse imagery, download what you need now and save the licence page alongside the files. When a public domain library disappears, nobody can take your rights to those files away, but you'll need the records to show where they came from.

### TheStocks.im, Jay Mantri and Superfamous

TheStocks.im used to pull together photos from a list of free sites in one place. It still loads, but it has relaunched as "The Stocks 3", a directory of more than 2,100 design tools rather than a photo aggregator. We cover what happened to each of its original sources in our [TheStocks.im alternatives guide](https://allthefreestock.com/blog/thestocks-im-alternatives/).

Two of those sources no longer offer free photos. `jaymantri.com` now shows one line about the photographer's design job, and only 7 of his photos remain on his [Unsplash profile](https://unsplash.com/@jaymantri). `superfamous.com` is now Folkert Gorter's studio portfolio with no image library, so his CC BY nature and abstract photos are no longer offered there.

## Sites that went dormant

A dormant site still loads but hasn't added anything in years. Its downloads usually still work. The risk is that a dormant site can vanish without notice, and its licence page can go with it.

- **[Little Visuals](https://littlevisuals.co/)** offered free high-resolution photo packs. Founder Nic Jackson died in November 2013, and his family has kept the site online, with its photos marked CC0.
- **[New Old Stock](https://nos.twnsnd.co/)**, Cole Townsend's collection of vintage photos from public archives, still loads on Tumblr. The newest post we found was dated 14 October 2022.
- **[Travel Coffee Book](https://travelcoffeebook.com/)** still shows travel photos with download links, but the newest posts are from September 2019. One download link pointed to an unrelated German shop domain, a common sign of an unmaintained site.
- **[Public Domain Archive](https://www.publicdomainarchive.com/)** says new photos are added weekly. Its newest image folders date from September 2017.
- **[Startup Stock Photos](https://startupstockphotos.com/)** is online. The newest upload paths we found were from October 2023.
- **[morgueFile](https://www.morguefile.com/)** still calls itself a free photo archive, but in October 2026 its homepage mostly promoted iStock subscriptions and we couldn't load its photo search.

> **Common mistake:** Bookmarking a dormant site's licence page and assuming it will always be there. Save a PDF or screenshot of the licence on the day you download, and note the date in your file name or asset sheet.

## Sites that went paid or changed model

Some sites are still live but no longer offer what made them popular. Usually a free offer turned into a free trial, or AI generation tools were added alongside the photos.

### Death to the Stock Photo

Death to the Stock Photo started in 2013 by emailing subscribers a free themed pack of 10 photos each month ([Fstoppers, 2014](https://fstoppers.com/originals/fstoppers-exclusive-interview-death-stock-photo-gives-subscribers-images-free-9498)). By February 2015 it also sold a US$10 a month premium library ([Cult of Mac](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/death-stock-aims-stock-photo-agency-like-no)). Today the [Death to Stock](https://deathtothestockphoto.com/) homepage leads with paid memberships from US$20 a month and a 14-day free trial. The free offer is now an email newsletter with "10+ free visuals".

### Picjumbo, Gratisography and SplitShire

These three are still free and still updated, with extra features:

- **[Picjumbo](https://picjumbo.com/)** offers free photos plus a PREMIUM membership for its 200+ themed collections.
- **[Gratisography](https://gratisography.com/)** still offers free photos without attribution and now has an "AI Generated" collection. If you need real photography, check each image's category.
- **[SplitShire](https://www.splitshire.com/)** calls itself a free stock photo and video platform, and now sells subscription plans for AI image, video and sound generation.

### Burst moved house

[Burst](https://www.shopify.com/stock-photos), Shopify's free photo library, hasn't closed. `burst.shopify.com` now redirects to `shopify.com/stock-photos`, and the photos are still free for commercial use without attribution under the [Burst terms](https://www.shopify.com/stock-photos/legal/terms). If your old links break, swap in the new address.

### Foodiesfeed and the free video sites

[Foodiesfeed](https://www.foodiesfeed.com/for-sale), the CC0 food photo site founded in Prague in 2014, is live but listed for sale. The listing says an eight-year Getty Images sponsorship ended in August 2026. Small free sites have often paid for hosting with sponsorship deals like that one.

Video went through the same consolidation. Freepik bought Videvo in June 2022, when it had more than 500,000 freemium clips and over 650,000 users ([BusinessCloud](https://businesscloud.co.uk/news/freepik-snaps-up-oxford-tech-firm-videvo)). Freepik [rebranded as Magnific](https://tech.eu/2026/04/28/freepik-rebrands-as-magnific-unifying-its-ai-creative-stack-as-enterprise-and-no-collar-growth-accelerates/) on 28 April 2026, and both `videvo.net` and `mazwai.com` now redirect to [Magnific's video library](https://www.magnific.com/videos). Free clips there require credit to the creator. For other free footage sources, see our guide to the [best free stock footage sites](https://allthefreestock.com/blog/best-free-stock-footage-sites/).

Creative Commons Search went the same way: since 13 December 2021 it has redirected to Openverse, run by the WordPress project ([WordPress Openverse team](https://make.wordpress.org/openverse/2021/12/13/cc-search-now-redirects-to-openverse/)).

## Big libraries that changed their terms

The largest free libraries are still running, but several have rewritten their licences over the years. A photo you downloaded in 2016 may have come under different terms from the same photo today.

| Library | Change | Date | What it means for you |
| - | - | - | - |
| Unsplash | Left `CC0` for the Unsplash License | June 2017 | Free use, but you can't build a competing service |
| Unsplash | Bought by Getty Images | March 2021 | Free library continued |
| Unsplash | Launched paid Unsplash+ | October 2022 | Paid images mixed into search |
| Pixabay | Left `CC0` for the Pixabay License | 9 January 2019 | No standalone resale of unaltered files |
| Pixabay | Bought by Canva (with Pexels) | May 2019 | Both said to stay free |
| Pixabay | Renamed Content License; `CC0` restored for pre-2019 uploads | 17 April 2023 | Older uploads are public domain again |

Sources: [Creative Commons](https://creativecommons.org/2017/06/22/unsplash/), [Unsplash License](https://unsplash.com/license), [Unsplash history](https://unsplash.com/history), [Getty Images Newsroom](https://newsroom.gettyimages.com/en/unsplash/unsplash-launches-unsplash-a-new-unlimited-subscription-offering-exclusive-content), [Wikipedia: Pixabay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pixabay), [Pixabay Content License](https://pixabay.com/service/license-summary/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/05/17/australias-design-unicorn-canva-picks-up-two-free-image-sharing-services-and-launches-new-photo-product).

When Unsplash left CC0, Creative Commons estimated about 200,000 images had been shared under it, and asked Unsplash to keep them marked as CC0 or publish an archive. Unsplash has grown a lot since then: from 10 photos on a Tumblr blog in May 2013 to 5 million images by April 2023, according to its own [company history](https://unsplash.com/history). When it launched Unsplash+ at an introductory US$7 a month in October 2022, it said its "open library isn't going anywhere" ([PetaPixel](https://petapixel.com/2022/10/10/unsplash-adds-a-paid-tier-one-year-after-getty-takeover/)).

> **Worth knowing:** Our guide to [free stock photo licences](https://allthefreestock.com/blog/free-stock-photo-licences-explained/) explains the practical difference between CC0 and custom licences like Unsplash's and Pixabay's, including what each one says about reselling unaltered files.

## Why free stock sites shut down

Free stock sites usually close for one of four reasons: running costs, falling demand, acquisition, or a founder moving on. The cases above show each of them.

**Running costs.** High-resolution downloads are expensive to serve, and AI scrapers have added to the load. The Wikimedia Foundation reported that the bandwidth used to download multimedia from its projects grew 50% from January 2024, and that at least 65% of its most resource-consuming traffic came from bots, which account for about 35% of pageviews ([Wikimedia Foundation, April 2025](https://diff.wikimedia.org/2025/04/01/how-crawlers-impact-the-operations-of-the-wikimedia-projects/)). Wikimedia is a large foundation. A one-person photo site has no such budget.

**Falling demand.** Nappy's closure notice blames lower demand for authentic images since generators arrived. Free sites earn from affiliate banners and sponsorships, and both depend on traffic. Fewer visitors means less money for hosting.

**Acquisition.** Big companies buy free libraries to send users toward paid ones. In 2009 iStock was open about stock.xchng being a "marketing vehicle". Getty bought Unsplash in 2021, Canva bought Pexels and Pixabay in 2019, and Freepik bought Videvo in 2022 and EyeEm in 2023. Some of those libraries thrived after the sale. Others, like FreeImages, eventually ended up pointing at the buyer's paid site.

**Founders moving on.** Many early sites were one photographer's side project. When the photographer changed jobs, as with Jay Mantri, or died, as with Little Visuals' Nic Jackson, the site stopped growing.

> **Rule of thumb:** If a free site depends on one person or one sponsor, treat it as temporary. Download what you need, keep the licence, and don't rely on it as your only source.

## What happens to your licence after a site closes?

For CC0 and Creative Commons downloads, your right to use an image you got while the licence applied continues after the site goes offline. Creative Commons says its licences "are not revocable": a licensor can stop distributing a work, but anyone who already has a copy can keep using it under the original terms ([Creative Commons FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)). The [CC0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/publicdomain/zero/1.0/legalcode.en) describes its waiver as "permanently, irrevocably and unconditionally" made. Plagiarism Today's analysis reaches the same conclusion: removing a licence can't stop people who already rely on it ([Plagiarism Today, 2014](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2014/12/09/revoking-creative-commons-licenses/)).

Custom licences depend on their wording. The [Unsplash License](https://unsplash.com/license) calls its grant "irrevocable". Other sites' terms may say nothing either way. In paid stock, EyeEm stated that licences sold before closure stayed valid. Where a licence is silent, ask a lawyer before using the image in high-stakes work. This post is general information, not legal advice.

Your licence only helps if you can prove it. When a site disappears, its licence page usually disappears too. Keep these records for every image you use commercially:

1. The image file as downloaded, with its original file name.
2. The source page URL and the photographer's name.
3. A dated PDF or screenshot of the licence page on the day you downloaded.
4. Where you used the image (page URL, campaign, client).

If you never saved a copy, look for an archived snapshot of the licence page on the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/) from around your download date.

## How to pick free stock sources that last

Choose sources with a funded owner, a written licence and a visible upload history. This checklist sorts durable sources from fragile ones:

| Check | Durable sign | Fragile sign |
| - | - | - |
| Owner | Company, foundation or public institution | Anonymous, or one hobbyist |
| Licence | Dedicated licence page, `CC0` or named licence | "Free to use" with no terms |
| Activity | Uploads in the last few months | Newest uploads years old |
| Revenue | Paid tier, sponsor or public funding | None visible |
| Links | Working photographer profiles | Broken or spam download links |
| Size | Tens of thousands of photos or more | A few hundred |

None of these signals is a guarantee. FreeImages had a big owner and still ended up as a redirect. Spreading your downloads across two or three strong libraries, plus an institutional archive, protects you better than any single choice.

> **Worth knowing:** Answer engines like ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews tend to cite pages with current, checked facts. Saijo George, the [AI SEO consultant in Melbourne](https://saijogeorge.com) who curates this site, notes that dead outbound links make a page look abandoned to readers and crawlers alike. Fixing old image credits is cheap and worth doing.
